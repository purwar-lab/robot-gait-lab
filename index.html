How the joints are mounted

Hip flexion and knee bend swing in the forward direction. A 2-DOF leg has a fixed 18° outward mount. A 3-DOF leg adds an active lateral hip joint. All forward-swing axes stay aligned as you change the plate shape.

What makes walking possible?

During stance, a foot stays planted while the body moves forward. During swing, it lifts and reaches for the next step. The shaded ground polygon connects the planted feet; the center dot is the body center projected onto the ground.

The support area is a geometric teaching aid. This model does not simulate gravity, tipping, friction, torque, or dynamic balance. Units are illustrative.

Supported code & motion model

Servo declarations, attach(), write(), writeMicroseconds(), delay(), initialized variables, assignments, and arithmetic. Setup runs once; loop repeats with persistent globals. Control flow and custom functions are not supported.

One-leg sketches hold the chassis still. Code playback interpolates continuously between samples. Whole-robot sketches estimate travel from the motion of retained ground contacts; the slip readout shows disagreement between feet. Generated gait previews use exact planted-foot trajectories. Arbitrary code may lift every foot or penetrate the ground; the model shows this rather than inventing balance.

Generate gait sketch defaults to finer writeMicroseconds() commands using the Servo library?s default 544?2400 us angle mapping. Whole-degree write() commands remain available, but their foot paths are less precise. This is a preview interpolation, not a physical servo response model.

Exported pins are placeholders. Match pins, servo orientation, neutral offsets, and travel limits to the actual hardware.
